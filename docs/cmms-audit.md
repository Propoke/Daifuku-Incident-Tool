# CMMS Audit — Functionality Gaps vs. Real-World Systems

Reviewed as: a CMMS implementation specialist, alongside a senior service technician/manager reading it the way they'd read any tool they're about to be handed on the floor. Benchmarked against what's genuinely standard in Fiix, UpKeep, Maintenance Connection, eMaint X4, IBM Maximo, and ServiceMax — not an exhaustive feature-parity chase, but "would a real shop notice this is missing on day one."

## What's actually strong (say this first, because it's true)

Before the gaps: the **configuration versioning system** (immutable `ConfigurationVersion`s, per-asset overrides without mutating the shared template, a work order permanently snapshotting what the asset was running when the work happened) is more rigorous than most commercial CMMS tools bother with — most competitors track "current config" as a mutable field and lose history the moment someone edits it. The **site-scoping model** (one access-control mechanism, ownership completely decoupled from visibility) is cleaner than what a lot of mid-tier CMMS products actually ship. And the **RBAC-to-the-model-permission-table** design means every feature added so far inherited real access control for free instead of being bolted on later. That's a genuinely good foundation to be building the rest of this on.

The gaps below are real, but none of them are architecture problems — they're missing *modules*, and the existing patterns (immutable audit models, site-scoped querysets, service-function-plus-admin-plus-verification) are exactly the right shape to build them in.

## Critical — a real shop would bounce off these in week one

### 1. No file/photo attachments anywhere
Zero `FileField`/`ImageField` in the entire codebase. A technician can't attach a photo of the failure, of the fix, of a torn label, of a nameplate for parts lookup — to a Work Order, an Asset, or a Permit. This is the single most universal feature across every commercial CMMS (Fiix, UpKeep, Maintenance Connection, all of them) and the top complaint a technician would raise in the first ten minutes of using this. It also blocks the PWA's most obvious real-world use: "scan the part, fix the machine, snap a photo of the repair, done."

**Proposal**: an `Attachment` model (generic FK to any of Asset/WorkOrder/PermitToWork/IncidentReport, uploaded_by, file, caption, uploaded_at — immutable like the rest of the audit-relevant records) backed by object storage, not local disk (the infra plan already flagged MinIO for exactly this and it was never built). Surface an upload control on the WorkOrder admin and — more importantly — on the PWA's ticket detail screen, since that's where a phone camera actually gets used. This also finally gives the infra plan's MinIO recommendation a real reason to exist.

### 2. No notifications — email or otherwise
Grepped the whole repo: no `send_mail`, no `EmailMessage`, no notification model, nothing. A ticket gets assigned and the assignee finds out only by opening the app and checking. PM auto-generates a work order and nobody is told it exists until someone happens to look at the dispatch board. An SLA breaches and `sla_summary()` will show it on a dashboard someone has to remember to open. Every real CMMS treats "tell the right person something happened" as core, not optional.

**Proposal**: minimum viable version is email — configure an SMTP backend (this needs to happen in the infra plan too, it was never provisioned), and hook `send_mail` into the points that already exist as natural triggers: `WorkOrder.save()` on assignment, `maintenance.services.generate_due_work_orders()` on PM-generated tickets, and a new daily Celery task (same pattern as PM generation) that emails SLA-breach and low-stock (`StockLevel.needs_reorder`) summaries to the relevant role. In-app notification (a simple `Notification` model + a bell icon) is the natural v2 once email proves the trigger points are right.

### 3. No structured checklists — PM and permits are free text
`PMSchedule.description` is one `TextField`. Real preventive maintenance is a list of discrete steps a technician checks off (torque to spec, inspect belt tension, verify E-stop) — not a paragraph they're supposed to remember. Same problem on `PermitToWork` (no LOTO step-by-step). Without this, "PM compliance" (which the dashboard already measures) only tells you the ticket got closed, not that the actual steps got done — which is exactly the gap an auditor or insurance inspector would flag.

**Proposal**: `ChecklistTemplate` (reusable, attached to a `PMSchedule` or standalone) with ordered `ChecklistItem`s (text, requires a numeric/pass-fail response); `WorkOrderChecklistResponse` recording what the technician actually entered per item, per work order — append-only, same `ImmutableModel` pattern used everywhere else. Surface it in the PWA ticket detail screen as the natural place a technician fills it out.

### 4. No meter/usage-based PM triggers
Explicitly deferred when PM scheduling was built ("depends on equipment reporting meter readings, which nothing does yet") — but for material handling/conveyor equipment specifically, usage-based PM (every N running hours or N cycles) is often *more* common than calendar-based, because duty cycle varies a lot more than the calendar does. This was flagged as a known gap at the time, not a surprise now, but it's a real one.

**Proposal**: an `AssetMeterReading` model (asset, meter type — hours/cycles, value, recorded_at, recorded_by) with manual entry as the v1 input method (a technician logs the odometer-equivalent reading, same as they'd log labor hours today); `PMSchedule` gains an optional `meter_interval` alongside the existing `interval_days`, and `generate_due_work_orders()` checks whichever trigger fires first. IoT-fed automatic readings are legitimately out of scope for now (no sensor integration exists or was asked for), but manual entry unblocks the whole feature cheaply.

### 5. Nothing to scan on the *asset* itself
The mobile app scans spare parts beautifully (barcode → stock → book onto a ticket). It has no equivalent for assets — `Asset.tag` is documented as "Asset tag / QR-code identifier" but nothing in the PWA actually scans it. A technician standing at a machine wants to scan its tag and land on "here's this asset's history, here's its open tickets, tap to report a new issue" — not search for it by name.

**Proposal**: reuse the exact scan flow already built for parts — add an asset-lookup-by-tag endpoint to `mobile_api`, and a second scan mode in the PWA (the camera/`BarcodeDetector` code is already there, this is a routing change plus one new view, not new scanning infrastructure) landing on the asset history page (`reporting/services.py`'s `asset_history()` already exists and already renders everything needed).

## Important — workable for a pilot, but a manager will ask about these within the first month

- ~~**No comment thread on a work order.**~~ — DONE. `WorkOrderComment` (append-only, same `ImmutableModel` pattern as `WorkOrderStatusChange`) is shown chronologically via a `WorkOrderCommentInline` on the WorkOrder admin (author auto-set through the same `save_formset()`-hook pattern used for Attachment/uploaded_by, since `save_new()` doesn't work on an inline), and via `GET`/`POST /api/mobile/workorders/<id>/comments/` + a Comments card on the PWA ticket detail screen — this was explicitly framed as a technician-on-a-phone workflow, so it isn't admin-only. RBAC: Admin/Incident Manager/Lead Technician/Technician get add+view, everyone else view-only; no change/delete grants anywhere, since the model itself refuses both. Verified against real Postgres: API list/create round-trip with the correct author, immutability enforced at the model layer (edit and delete both raise), the admin inline auto-sets author on a real POST, and a view-only role (Management) gets 200 on GET but 403 on POST.
- ~~**No vendor/supplier model.**~~ / ~~**No purchase-order/procurement workflow.**~~ — DONE, together. `assets.Vendor` (name, contact, `lead_time_days`) is a real record `SparePart.vendor` now references (the old free-text `SparePart.supplier` stays, for parts not yet assigned a real vendor). `inventory.PurchaseOrder`/`PurchaseOrderLine` give `StockLevel.needs_reorder` an actual next step: a PO against a vendor, lines per spare part, and a receiving workflow where raising `PurchaseOrderLine.quantity_received` (cumulative, like `AssetMeterReading.value` - the increase *is* the receiving action, not a delta you compute yourself) adds the delta straight to the matching `StockLevel` and rolls the parent PO's status up through `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`/`RECEIVED` automatically - all in `PurchaseOrderLine.save()`, so it works identically whether you're editing the admin inline or calling it from code, no `save_formset` workaround needed here since raising a real form field (not an implicit stamped one) is a completely different, already-working code path. `quantity_received` can never be decreased once recorded (would mean un-receiving stock already added). RBAC: Admin/Spare Parts Manager get full CRUD on `Vendor`/`PurchaseOrder`/`PurchaseOrderLine`, everyone else view-only. Verified against real Postgres: a `StockLevel` doesn't exist until the first receipt, a partial receive adds exactly the right quantity and flips the PO to `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`, completing the order flips it to `RECEIVED`, decreasing `quantity_received` and over-receiving both correctly raise, and the real admin form creates a PO+line with `created_by` auto-set to the logged-in user.
- ~~**No technician skill/certification tracking.**~~ — DONE. `teams.Certification` (holder, name, issued_date, `expiry_date` - blank means it doesn't expire) is a general skills/quals roster, not LOTO-specific. `safety.admin.PermitToWorkAdmin.save_model()` checks it when a permit with `lockout_tagout_applied=True` is issued: if `issued_to` doesn't hold a current (`expiry_date` blank or in the future) certification named exactly `"LOTO Authorized"`, the permit is still issued but a warning message is shown - a real gap surfaced, not a hard block, per the proposal. `name` isn't a fixed enum since shops vary what they call things, so this specific check needs that exact string; documented in code. RBAC: Admin/Management author certification records, everyone else view-only. Verified against real Postgres through the actual admin form: no certification on file shows the warning, a current one suppresses it, an expired one brings it back, and the permit is issued in all three cases (never blocked).
- ~~**No labor cost rate.**~~ — DONE. `Team.hourly_rate` is the default; `WorkOrderLaborEntry.hourly_rate` optionally overrides it per entry (contractor labor, or a team with no rate set) - same nullable-defers-to-a-fallback convention already used for `SparePart.unit_cost`/`parts_cost_total`. `reporting.services.labor_cost_total()` sums `hours × (entry rate, else team rate, else 0)` and is now on the dashboard (`labor_cost_total`, alongside the pre-existing `labor_hours_total`) and its template. Entries with no rate anywhere still count toward hours, just contribute $0 to cost - deliberately not treated as an error, since not every shop rate-tracks everyone. Verified against real Postgres: a team-rate entry, a per-entry override, and a no-rate entry summed to exactly the expected total ($50×2h + $100×1h + $0×3h = $200), and the dashboard page renders the new stat.
- ~~**No approval/sign-off workflow.**~~ — DONE. `PermitToWork` gains `approved_by`/`approved_at`, but neither is a plain editable form field (`readonly_fields` in `PermitToWorkAdmin`) - the only way to set them is a dedicated "Approve selected permits" admin action, gated on a new custom permission (`safety.can_approve_permittowork`, declared via the model's own `Meta.permissions` and granted only to Admin/Management/Incident Manager). Re-running the action on an already-approved permit reports it as already approved rather than re-stamping it. Verified against real Postgres through the actual admin: a Technician has the permission denied (correctly `False`) and is blocked from approving even by directly POSTing the action; an Incident Manager has it granted and a real approval sets both fields; a second run doesn't double-approve; and `approved_by` renders as read-only display text on the change form, never an editable input.
- **No backorder/"waiting on parts" state.** When `consume_stock()` hits insufficient stock it just raises an error — there's no way to flag "this job is blocked on a part" and have the work order reflect that (versus just sitting in `ON_HOLD` with no reason recorded).
- ~~**No global search.**~~ — DONE. `core.search.global_search()` + `/search/` (linked from the home page nav) searches assets (tag/name/serial number), work orders (title/description/asset tag), and spare parts (SKU/barcode/description) from one box. Assets and work orders are site-scoped the same way every other view in this repo is (`scope_queryset_to_sites`); the spare part catalog itself is global, matching the existing treatment in `mobile_api.SparePartLookupView` - only stock levels are site-scoped, not the catalog. Results link to the existing asset-history page and the relevant admin change pages rather than duplicating those views. Verified against real Postgres: finds an accessible asset by tag, correctly omits a cross-site asset a technician has no access to (fail-closed, same as everywhere else site-scoping is enforced), finds a part by barcode, finds a work order by a title fragment, and an empty query renders no result tables.
- **No warranty tracking on internally-owned assets.** `ServiceContract` covers customer-owned equipment; nothing tracks warranty expiry for assets *we* own. Cheap to add (`warranty_expiry` on `Asset`), easy to miss until someone needs it during a warranty dispute.

## Lower priority — real gaps, but a pilot doesn't live or die on them

- **OEE (availability × performance × quality)** — standard in manufacturing-flavored CMMS, but needs the meter-reading infrastructure above as a prerequisite anyway.
- **No custom/ad-hoc report builder or CSV export** — the dashboard is fixed-metric; exporting to spreadsheet is a common ask once someone wants to slice the data their own way.
- **No calendar export (iCal) for dispatch/PM schedules** — a small quality-of-life add once the dispatch board sees real use.
- **No work-order relationships** (split into sub-tasks, duplicate-of, blocks/blocked-by) — matters more at higher ticket volume than a pilot will hit.
- **No parts kitting** (pre-bundled sets of parts for a common job type) — an efficiency feature, not a functionality gap.
- **No customer e-signature / sign-off in the portal** — relevant once field-service billing/proof-of-service actually matters to the business, not before.
- **In-app push notifications on the PWA** — the same trigger points as the email proposal above, once that exists.

## What I'm *not* re-flagging here

Already documented as known, deliberate gaps elsewhere in this repo, not new findings: PWA offline queueing and real-device camera testing (`docs/mobile-app-backlog.md`), SLA auto-escalation/business-hours awareness (`workorders/sla.py`), customer-owned-asset scoping boundaries, and portal password-security hardening (`docs/cmms-feature-draft.md`). Repeating them here would just be padding the list.

## Proposed order of attack

If I were sequencing this the way the rest of this build has gone (small, verified, real-Postgres-tested slices — not a big-bang rewrite):

1. ~~**Attachments**~~ — DONE. `core.Attachment` (generic FK, local-disk `FileField`, RBAC via a dedicated migration: add/view for Technician/Lead Technician, view-only for Management/Asset Manager/Spare Parts Manager/OEM, delete restricted to Admin/Incident Manager) is wired into Asset, WorkOrder, PermitToWork, and IncidentReport admin via a shared `AttachmentInline`. `uploaded_by` is auto-set through a `SetsAttachmentUploaderMixin` on the parent ModelAdmins (`save_formset()` — not `save_new()` on the inline, which Django never calls on an `InlineModelAdmin`). Verified against real Postgres: upload through the WorkOrder admin form, file lands on disk, `uploaded_by` set to the logged-in technician, delete correctly 403s/hides the delete checkbox for a plain Technician. PWA ticket-detail upload UI and object storage (MinIO) are still open — this closed the admin-side gap only.
2. ~~**Email notifications**~~ — DONE. `core.notifications.notify_work_order_assigned()` fires from `WorkOrder.save()` whenever `assigned_to` or `assigned_team` changes (covers both direct admin reassignment and PM auto-generated tickets, which go through the same `save()`), emailing the assignee and/or all team members with an email address. `core.notifications.notify_daily_digest()` emails an SLA-breach summary (`workorders.sla.breached_open_work_orders()`, new - covers both "breached" and "overdue" per `get_sla_status`'s own distinction) to Management/Incident Manager, and a low-stock summary to Spare Parts Manager, on a Celery beat schedule seeded the same way as PM generation (`core/migrations/0011_seed_daily_digest_task.py`, admin-editable cadence). `EMAIL_BACKEND` defaults to Django's console backend so this works with zero setup locally; real SMTP is one env var away (`.env.example`) once a relay is provisioned. Verified against real Postgres/locmem: individual assignment, team assignment, no re-notification on unrelated saves, SLA-breach digest, and low-stock digest all confirmed to send the right email to the right recipients with real fixture data.
3. ~~**Asset QR scan-to-open in the PWA**~~ — DONE. `GET /api/mobile/assets/lookup/?code=<tag>` (`mobile_api`, site-scoped like every other endpoint here, reuses the existing `assets.view_asset` permission - no new RBAC migration needed) resolves a scanned tag to an asset id; the PWA's Scan tab now has a part/asset mode toggle, and an asset match hands off via `window.location.href` to the existing site-scoped `reporting.views.asset_history_view` page rather than building a second asset-detail screen. Verified against real Postgres: own-site lookup returns 200, a tag at a site the technician has no access to correctly 404s (and starts working once access is granted), a missing `code` param 400s, and the target history page renders.
4. **Work order comments** — small, same pattern as `WorkOrderStatusChange`, closes a real daily-workflow gap.
5. ~~**Structured checklists**~~ — DONE (WorkOrder side; permit response-capture still open, see below). `core.ChecklistTemplate`/`ChecklistItem` (ordered steps, each PASS_FAIL/NUMERIC/TEXT) are reusable and attach to a `PMSchedule` or `PermitToWork`; `PMSchedule.checklist_template` is copied onto the generated `WorkOrder` by `maintenance.services.generate_due_work_orders()` the same way `assigned_team` already is. `WorkOrderChecklistResponse` (append-only, same `ImmutableModel` pattern as `WorkOrderComment`) records what a technician actually entered per item, per work order - a corrected answer is a new row, not an edit, so the full attempt history (failed, fixed, re-checked) stays visible; callers wanting "the current answer" take the latest response per item. Surfaced via a `WorkOrderChecklistResponseInline` on the WorkOrder admin (dropdown restricted to that work order's own template's items) and `GET`/`POST /api/mobile/workorders/<id>/checklist/` plus a Checklist card on the PWA ticket detail screen. `PermitToWork.checklist_template` exists for the step-by-step LOTO procedure to display, but response capture is WorkOrder-only in this pass - a permit-level equivalent is a known follow-up. PM-compliance reporting also doesn't yet factor in checklist completion (still measures "ticket closed," not "steps done") - flagged as a further follow-up, not built here. Verified against real Postgres: GET on a ticket with no template returns `[]`, a fresh checklist shows all items unanswered, a FAIL then a corrected PASS both persist as separate rows while GET correctly shows only the latest, the admin inline's item dropdown is genuinely restricted to the right template, `completed_by`/`author` auto-population works through the real admin form, and PM auto-generation correctly copies `checklist_template` onto the new work order.
6. ~~**Meter-based PM triggers**~~ — DONE. `assets.AssetMeterReading` (append-only, same `ImmutableModel` pattern as everything else logged here; manual entry via a new `AssetMeterReadingAdmin` for v1, same as labor hours are logged today) records a cumulative odometer-style reading per asset/meter type (hours or cycles). `PMSchedule` gained an optional `meter_type`/`meter_interval` (denormalized `next_due_meter_value`, same anchor-and-advance shape as `next_due_date`) alongside the existing calendar interval; `generate_due_work_orders()` now checks whichever trigger fires first, advances whichever one(s) fired, and still calendar-narrows its query at the DB level (`next_due_date__lte=as_of OR has a meter trigger configured`) rather than scanning every active schedule regardless of relevance - a true meter-due check still needs a per-row lookup of the asset's latest reading, which isn't expressible as a single indexed comparison, so this is a known, accepted tradeoff at the scale this system targets. Verified against real Postgres: a meter-only schedule with no readings yet doesn't fire, a reading below threshold doesn't fire, a reading at/above threshold fires exactly once and advances the trigger past it, a re-run without a new reading doesn't re-fire, the pre-existing calendar-only path is unaffected (regression check), `AssetMeterReading` is immutable (edit/delete both raise), and the admin form auto-sets `recorded_by` to the logged-in technician.
7. ~~Vendor model → PO workflow, technician certifications, labor rates, approval workflow, global search~~ — ALL DONE, in that order. See the individually-checked-off bullets above for each one's detail and verification.
